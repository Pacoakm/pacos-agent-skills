# Visual direction library

Use these as ingredients, not a menu to repeat verbatim. Tailor 4–6 choices to the subject and explain why each fits. Combine no more than two primary directions so the result retains a clear identity.

## 1. Archive press × industrial cutaway

- **Look:** aged newspapers, halftone photography, technical patent drawings, oil-stained steel, torn orange-red paper.
- **Palette:** dark brown, charcoal, warm paper, oxidized orange, restrained cream.
- **Type:** condensed grotesk headlines plus monospaced technical labels.
- **Motion:** printing-press slams, mechanical iris reveals, rotating exploded views, paper layers on multiplane depth, gear-driven wipes.
- **Best for:** technology history, competition, manufacturing, geopolitics, “war” or rivalry narratives.

## 2. Stop-motion paper laboratory

- **Look:** hand-cut paper, registration marks, pencil annotations, tactile shadows, miniature physical sets.
- **Palette:** subject-led paper colors over an off-white or black cutting mat.
- **Type:** editorial sans with occasional stamped labels.
- **Motion:** stepped animation, replacement shapes, fold/unfold reveals, match cuts through punched holes, top-down camera pushes.
- **Best for:** education, comedy, approachable science, abstract ideas that need charm.

## 3. Blueprint X-ray noir

- **Look:** translucent components, wireframes, blueprints, scan lines, volumetric shafts, black laboratory space.
- **Palette:** black, gunmetal, electric cyan, ultraviolet, one warning color.
- **Type:** precise technical grotesk and numeric readouts.
- **Motion:** X-ray sweeps, sectional travel-throughs, focus pulls across layers, orbiting callouts, aperture masks.
- **Best for:** hardware anatomy, engineering, medicine, systems, “how it works.”

## 4. Luxury editorial macro

- **Look:** extreme close-ups, premium materials, restrained grids, crisp negative space, soft controlled reflections.
- **Palette:** near-black, warm white, one brand accent, metallic highlights.
- **Type:** elegant display face paired with neutral sans.
- **Motion:** slow macro dolly, precision rack focus, object match cuts, light sweeps, typography locked to surfaces.
- **Best for:** product launches, brand films, premium service promotion.

## 5. Retro-future broadcast control room

- **Look:** CRT phosphor, analog meters, radar scopes, chunky switches, broadcast graphics, chromatic misregistration.
- **Palette:** deep navy or brown-black, phosphor green, amber, signal red.
- **Type:** broadcast condensed faces, seven-segment numerals, ticker text.
- **Motion:** signal tuning, scanline wipes, dial rotations, snap zooms, screens becoming portals.
- **Best for:** fast explainers, history, media, finance, playful product demos.

## 6. Spatial data theatre

- **Look:** data and diagrams staged as architecture in a deep 2.5D/3D environment.
- **Palette:** disciplined dark field with luminous categorical colors.
- **Type:** minimal neutral sans, large numeric anchors.
- **Motion:** camera flies through charts, scale transitions from macro to micro, data points become objects, seamless object portals.
- **Best for:** comparisons, performance claims, timelines, business or scientific data.

## 7. Museum dossier collage

- **Look:** archival photographs, evidence tags, cotton gloves, specimen trays, maps, handwritten marginalia.
- **Palette:** parchment, ink black, museum green, sealing-wax red.
- **Type:** serif captions with restrained utilitarian labels.
- **Motion:** table-top tracking, evidence-card flips, magnifier reveals, map-to-object match cuts, layered rack focus.
- **Best for:** documentary, biography, mystery, cultural and historical subjects.

## 8. Clean kinetic systems

- **Look:** immaculate vector geometry, modular grids, soft depth, precise diagrams, minimal texture.
- **Palette:** off-white or deep charcoal with a compact accessible brand palette.
- **Type:** modern sans with clear scale contrast.
- **Motion:** shared-axis transitions, shape morphs, masked type, diagram-to-diagram continuity, purposeful spring dynamics.
- **Best for:** tutorials, SaaS explainers, classroom content, interfaces, complex sequences needing maximum clarity.

## How to present choices

For each proposed direction, give:

1. A short memorable name.
2. A one-sentence visual promise.
3. Palette and material cues.
4. Three signature camera/transition moves.
5. One tradeoff, such as production complexity, reduced warmth, or lower suitability for dense text.

Recommend one direction based on the brief, but let the user decide. If combining directions, declare one dominant system and one accent system.
